\noindent
I grew most as a leader when I served as a teaching assistant for an introductory software engineering course. Early on, I noticed a recurring pattern in team-based projects: confident students quickly took ownership of core coding decisions, while quieter or less experienced classmates drifted into ``safe'' tasks and spoke less over time. I realized that helping students learn wasn't only about answering technical questions---it was also about shaping a team environment where everyone could practice real engineering work.

\par\noindent
To address this, I introduced lightweight structure: role rotation so each student owned a meaningful component at least once; short, weekly check-ins where every member had to explain one design choice; and ``pair-debugging'' sessions that normalized asking for help. I also changed how I gave feedback---focusing on concrete next steps and celebrating small wins to build confidence.

\par\noindent
By the end of the term, students who were initially hesitant began leading demos, proposing architectures, and teaching teammates. That experience changed my professional mindset: I want to build systems, but I also want to build communities where people feel capable of doing hard things together. At Northwestern, I hope to keep mentoring in courses, project teams, and student groups in the same spirit.
